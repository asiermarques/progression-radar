key: ai
name: AI Systems
description: Designs, builds, evaluates and operates AI-powered systems for products and users, from model integration and retrieval to inference, agents and reusable platform capabilities
---

## Level 1

### Understands model capabilities and limitations

**Example behaviours:**

- Explains the basic concepts of ML and language models, including inference, tokens, context windows and non-deterministic outputs
- Recognises common model failure modes, including hallucinations, incorrect reasoning and unreliable outputs
- Uses existing AI services or model APIs for simple tasks and validates their results

### Writes and tests effective model instructions

**Example behaviours:**

- Writes clear prompts with appropriate context, instructions, examples and output formats
- Tests model behaviour against representative examples and identifies obvious failures

## Level 2

### Integrates AI capabilities into products

**Example behaviours:**

- Builds AI-powered features using model APIs, structured outputs and appropriate context or retrieval techniques
- Handles model limitations, API failures and unreliable outputs using established patterns
- Understands when to use prompting, retrieval, conventional software or other approaches

### Evaluates AI feature quality

**Example behaviours:**

- Creates representative evaluation cases from real product scenarios
- Measures correctness, latency and cost, and identifies regressions when prompts or models change
- Uses evaluation results to improve features before releasing them

## Level 3

### Designs and operates reliable AI systems

**Example behaviours:**

- Designs or critically reviews AI architectures, including inference, retrieval and agent-based workflows when appropriate
- Establishes monitoring, evaluation and recovery mechanisms for AI-specific failures
- Defines appropriate fallback, human-review and deployment strategies based on the risks of the use case
- Establishes LLMOps practices for production AI systems, including versioning, tracing, continuous evaluation, monitoring and safe rollback.

### Selects models and techniques based on evidence

**Example behaviours:**

- Compares models using representative evaluations, balancing quality, latency, cost and reliability
- Evaluates architectural alternatives, including prompting, RAG, fine-tuning and traditional ML approaches
- Identifies the causes of quality problems and guides improvements to models, data, prompts or system design

## Level 4

### Designs AI platforms and shared architectures

**Example behaviours:**

- Designs reusable components for multiple teams, such as model gateways, inference services, evaluation platforms or model registries
- Establishes patterns for model lifecycle management, observability, deployment and resilience
- Optimises system-wide quality, latency, reliability and cost while reducing duplication across teams
- Designs reusable LLMOps capabilities, including evaluation pipelines, prompt and model registries, AI gateways and production observability.

### Establishes evaluation and operational standards

**Example behaviours:**

- Defines evaluation practices combining automated metrics, model-based evaluation, human review and production signals
- Establishes standards for deploying, monitoring and updating AI capabilities safely
- Enables teams to develop and operate AI features independently using shared capabilities

## Level 5

### Defines long-term AI architecture and technical strategy

**Example behaviours:**

- Defines architectural principles and the long-term technical direction for AI systems across products and teams
- Evaluates build-versus-buy decisions, model providers and technology investments based on business and technical trade-offs
- Anticipates changes in model capabilities and evolves the architecture to take advantage of them
- Establishes organisation-wide technical standards for AI system quality, reliability, interoperability and lifecycle management
- Influences strategic decisions by explaining technical opportunities, limitations and risks to leadership
